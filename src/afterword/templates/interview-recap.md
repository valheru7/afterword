---
name: interview-recap
description: A voice memo you record right after an interview that wasn't recorded. Turns your rambling recap into interview notes and a thank-you draft.
---
This is not a conversation. It's a voice memo the candidate recorded alone, right after a job interview, talking through what happened from memory. Every speaker label is the candidate. Anything "they" said is the candidate's recollection, not a quote, so never present it as the interviewer's exact words.

The memo will jump around, repeat itself and correct itself ("wait, no, that was the second guy"). Go with the latest correction. Write notes the candidate will use to prepare for later rounds and to follow up.

Use these sections, in this order. Leave a section out if the memo didn't cover it.

## Summary
Two or three sentences: who the candidate met, what kind of interview it was (recruiter screen, hiring manager, technical, panel, culture), and how the candidate felt it went.

## People
Each interviewer's name, role and anything notable the candidate mentioned. Don't guess at names that weren't given.

## Questions they asked
Each question the candidate remembers, then a short note on how they say they answered.

## How the answers landed
Honest, specific feedback based on what the candidate described: answers they felt good about, answers they wish they'd done differently, and what to prepare or back up with a concrete example next time. Be kind but don't flatter. If the candidate is being hard on themselves without reason, say so.

## About the role
Team, scope, tech stack, responsibilities, location or remote policy, compensation, benefits, and any green or red flags the candidate mentioned. Only include what the memo actually says.

## Questions I asked
The candidate's questions and the answers they remember getting.

## Next steps
Timeline, who reaches out next, upcoming rounds, and anything the candidate promised to send.

## Thank-you note
A short, warm thank-you email (under 120 words) to the main interviewer. Mention one specific thing the candidate remembered from the conversation. No clichés, no em dashes.
